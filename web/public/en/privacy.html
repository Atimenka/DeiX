This document describes how the website and the OS actually work. The lists of data, cookies and tables were checked against the source code and the database migrations in the project repository; when the code changes, the policy is updated together with it.

1. General provisions

1.1. This policy applies to the website deix-sepia.vercel.app and its mirror deix-atimenkas-projects.vercel.app (the one the OS itself talks to), to the English version under /en, to the programming interface /api/* and to the online features of the DeiX OS operating system: dev-mode key requests, linking a device to an account, the update center and image downloads. All of this is called the “Service” below.

1.2. The operator of the Service is the project owner, a private individual who runs the project under the name Atimenka (GitHub profile: github.com/Atimenka). DeiX OS is an independent open-source project, not a company: we sell nothing, show no advertising and do not make money from data.

1.3. By registering on the website and entering data into its forms you confirm that you have read this policy. If you disagree, do not create an account: browsing the pages, downloading published images and running the OS itself do not require an account. An account is needed for dev-mode key requests and for linking a device.

1.4. The main principle: the server keeps only what the Service needs to protect itself (email, name, role, password hash, service records). Everything else that concerns you personally and is not needed by the server is kept on your side, encrypted (section 7).

1.5. The policy is written with the general principles of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and of the Russian Federal Law No. 152-FZ “On Personal Data” in mind: lawfulness, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity and confidentiality. You may exercise the rights in section 13 regardless of your country of residence.

2. In short

  • Your password is stored only as an irreversible hash. Nobody can read it, including the operator.
  • The server holds your email, username, role, password hash and service records needed for security (section 4 and the appendix).
  • The names of your devices and the history of issued keys are stored only in your browser, encrypted with your password. They never reach the server.
  • No advertising, trackers, analytics or third-party scripts. The single exception is the Cloudflare Turnstile captcha on registration and sign-in.
  • Cookies are only those needed to sign in. There is no consent banner for them, because refusing them would make signing in impossible.
  • Data is not sold and is not passed to advertising networks or data brokers.
  • The OS sends no telemetry. It contacts the project servers only on your commands and to poll the status of your request (section 15).
  • You can have data deleted on request (section 14). Some data you delete yourself: clearing browser storage, resetting the vault on the Account page, turning off 2FA.

3. Who processes data

3.1. The operator decides which data is needed and why. The operator can see the data that exists in the project database and uses it only to run the Service and to keep it secure.

3.2. Infrastructure providers process data on behalf of the operator: Vercel (hosting of the site and functions), Supabase (database, accounts, file storage), Cloudflare (Turnstile captcha). If the operator turns on email sending, the mail service Resend or Brevo is added. Details are in section 10.

3.3. The Owner role in the website interface is the operator. Other users see only their own data and the public pages.

4. What data we process

4.1. Account

When you register you provide a username (3–24 characters: Latin letters, digits, underscore), an email address and a password (8–72 characters). The Service stores: the email address, the username, the role (user or owner), the creation date, the “email verified” flag and the password hash (created and stored by Supabase Auth; the password itself is never saved). The password confirmation field exists only to catch typos and is not saved.

The username is unique and is visible to the operator in the request list. The email is not shown to the operator in the request list, but is available to the operator as the database administrator.

4.2. Two-factor authentication

If you turn on TOTP, Supabase Auth stores the one-time code secret (the factor). The Service also creates 8 backup codes; the database holds only their SHA-256 hashes and a used marker. The codes themselves are shown to you once, when they are created. Two-factor authentication is mandatory for the Owner.

4.3. Email verification

For the verification link the Service creates a random token. The database holds its SHA-256 hash, the expiry (24 hours) and the time of use; the token itself exists only in the email. See section 5.

4.4. Device linking

To link a device to an account the OS creates a device identifier (32 random hexadecimal characters; it is not derived from serial numbers or other hardware traits) and receives a short code. You enter that code on the Account page. The Service stores the device identifier, before linking the code and its expiry (10 minutes), and after linking the account and the time of linking.

4.5. Dev-mode key requests

A request stores: the device identifier, the device name (hostname), a note (up to 280 characters), the source (os or website), the status, the times of submission and decision, the name of the Owner who decided, the time the key was used and your account. These fields are addressed to the operator who makes the decision, so they stay on the server. Do not put personal or sensitive data into the note or the device name: the operator will read it.

4.6. Key issuance counter

To limit key issuance (no more than 5 per hour per account) the Service stores the account identifier and the time of issuance. Which device and which one-time number went into a key is not stored by the server: the key is signed and is verified in the OS without a network, and the issuance history is kept in your encrypted vault (section 7).

4.7. Published images and updates

OS images and update packages are published by the Owner. The database stores the version, description, size, checksums, file path, download counter and the name of the Owner who created the record. The download counter is a total and is not tied to a person.

4.8. Technical connection data

With every request to the site your browser or the OS transmits an IP address, a User-Agent string and the time of the request. This data inevitably passes through the Vercel infrastructure (and Cloudflare when the captcha is shown). The Service uses the IP address to protect against abuse: it limits the number of registrations, failed sign-ins, email verifications, downloads and update requests. These counters live in the memory of the function instance and disappear when it stops; the IP address is not written to the database. The operator keeps no logs of IP addresses of his own and no “profiles” of visitors. The logs of Vercel itself are governed by its policy and plan (section 10).

4.9. What we do not collect

  • Payment data, passports and other documents, phone numbers, precise location.
  • The contents of your files and any information about how you use the OS on your computer.
  • Data for advertising and profiling, browser fingerprints, information about other sites you visit.
  • Data about children: the Service is not intended for them (section 16).

5. Email verification

5.1. Domain check at registration (always active). The Service rejects addresses from a list of disposable mail services and checks through DNS that the domain after the “@” has a mail server (an MX record, or an IPv4 address record if there is none). For this the server function sends the public DNS resolver only the domain part of the address; the local part (before the “@”) is not transmitted. If the DNS query itself fails because of an outage, the address is not rejected: a resolver failure does not prove that there is no mail. This check does not prove that the mailbox exists or belongs to you.

5.2. Verification link email (active if the operator turned sending on). After registration an email with a link is sent to the address you gave. The token in the link is random (256 bits), valid for 24 hours and works once; only its hash is stored in the database. The mail is sent by the mail service Resend or Brevo in the language of the page you registered on: it receives your address, your username and the text of the message. You can request another email in your account no more than three times per hour.

5.3. What verification does. While email sending is not turned on, verification blocks nothing. When it is on, unverified accounts do not receive dev-mode keys and cannot submit requests. Verification does not block signing in.

5.4. Which emails we send. Only service messages: the verification link. There are no newsletters, news or advertising, and there will be none without your separate consent.

7. Encrypted browser vault

7.1. Data that concerns you personally and is not needed by the server for security (the names of your devices and the history of the dev keys issued to you) is stored as one block in your browser’s localStorage. The block is encrypted with AES-256-GCM; the key is derived from your password with PBKDF2-SHA256 (600,000 iterations, random salt) and is never sent to the server.

7.2. The key is created at sign-in or registration and until sign-out is kept in IndexedDB as a non-extractable key: the site pages reload on every navigation, and asking for the password on each of them would be impractical. Signing out or losing the session deletes the key; the data stays encrypted.

7.3. The block is bound to the account identifier, so substitution or corruption of the data can be detected. When you change your password on the site, the block is re-encrypted with the new password. If the password was changed in another browser, the data here stays under the old password: you can open it with the old password or reset it on the Account page.

7.4. The operator cannot read this vault and cannot recover it. If you cleared the site data or moved to another computer, the vault will be empty.

7.5. Limitation: while you are signed in, a script of this same site can use the key. The protection is a strict content security policy (CSP: script-src 'self') and the absence of external scripts other than the captcha.

9. How we protect data

  • Passwords: stored only as hashes (Supabase Auth); a wrong password at sign-in gets the same answer and an artificial pause, so as not to reveal whether an account exists.
  • Connection: all traffic uses HTTPS; cookies carry the Secure, HttpOnly and SameSite=Strict flags.
  • Strict CSP: only the site’s own scripts and styles and the captcha script are allowed; embedding the site in frames is forbidden.
  • Brute-force protection: a captcha on registration and sign-in, limits on registrations, failed sign-ins, 2FA codes, emails and downloads, and a hidden trap field for bots.
  • 2FA: TOTP optional for everyone and mandatory for the Owner; backup codes are stored as hashes and work once.
  • Database: row-level security (RLS) of Supabase is enabled for the tables; the full-access server key is kept only in the function settings and never reaches the browser.
  • Files: images and updates are in private storage; a download link is signed and valid for 5 minutes.
  • Signed updates and keys: the OS accepts an update and a dev-mode key only with a digital signature (ECDSA P-256); verification happens on your computer.
  • Minimisation: what the server does not need to know is not sent to it (section 7).

No system is absolutely secure. We do not promise the impossible, but we apply the measures above and fix vulnerabilities we find.

13.2. What you do yourself: change the password, turn 2FA on and off and regenerate backup codes on the Account page under “Security”; reset the encrypted vault on the Account page (the button appears when the vault is locked); clear the site data in your browser.

13.3. How to contact the operator: the ways are given in section 20. We must make sure the request comes from the account owner: we will answer to the account email or ask you to confirm the request from your session. We do not give out other people’s data.

13.4. Deadlines: we respond within the deadlines set by the applicable law (in the EU, one month, extendable; in Russia, ten working days, extendable), and in any case aim to do so within 30 days. Requests are free unless they are manifestly unfounded or excessive.

14. Deleting your account and data

14.1. There is no “Delete account” button in the website interface yet: deletion is done on a request to the operator (section 20). The request must arrive in a way that lets us confirm you own the account.

14.2. When an account is deleted, the profile, device links, key issuance counter, email verification tokens and 2FA backup code hashes are deleted with it, together with the Supabase Auth record containing the password hash and the 2FA factor.

14.3. Dev-mode key requests are detached from the account when it is deleted, but the row (device identifier, device name, note, status) may remain in the database. If you want it deleted as well, include the device identifier in your request: we will delete the record.

14.4. The operator cannot delete data in your browser: clear it yourself (section 6.5). Deleting the account does not revoke a dev-mode key already issued to you: it is signed and verified in the OS without a network.

14.5. We may retain a minimum of data if the law obliges us to keep it longer or it is needed to protect against abuse and to settle disputes, and only for as long as necessary.

15. DeiX OS and the network

15.1. No telemetry. The OS does not send the project servers information about how you use it, your files or your list of programs, and does not collect diagnostics without your knowledge.

15.2. Requests to the project servers happen only in connection with features you invoke yourself:

  • dev request sends the device identifier, the device name (hostname) and a source marker. After that the OS periodically polls the server for the status of the request (this is needed to show you a notification of approval) and stops when the status is final or after several failures.
  • dev link and linking by code send the device identifier in order to obtain a short code for linking to an account.
  • update check, update download and the “Updates” window request a signed manifest and chunks of the package. The server sees the IP address, the channel and the number of the requested build; nothing from the contents of your disk is transmitted.
  • dev unlock verifies the signature of the key locally, without a network.

15.3. All requests use an encrypted connection (TLS) through the /api/os gateway, so the OS does not contact Supabase directly.

15.4. Everything you keep in the OS (including the /userdata partition) stays on your computer and is encrypted with a key derived from your password. The project servers do not receive this data.

15.5. Other network features of the OS (for example the built-in browser and the curl command) contact directly the sites that you open; we do not know the privacy policies of those sites and are not responsible for them.

16. Children

The Service is not intended for persons under 16 and we knowingly do not collect their data. If you are a parent or guardian and believe a child created an account, write to us (section 20): we will delete the data. 17. Automated decisions

The Service makes no decisions that have legal effects for you solely by automated means and does no profiling. Only security measures act automatically: the captcha, request rate limits and the rejection of disposable email addresses. If the protection rejected you by mistake, write to us. Dev-mode key requests are reviewed by a human, the Owner.

18. Security incidents

The notice will say which data is affected and what we recommend you do (for example, change your password, regenerate backup codes).

21. Terms

Personal data
any information by which a person can be identified directly or indirectly: email, username, an IP address combined with an account.
Hash
an irreversible “fingerprint” of data: it lets you check a match but not recover the original value.
Token
a random string that confirms an action or a session.
2FA / TOTP
signing in with a password and a one-time code from an authenticator app.
CSP
content security policy: the browser may run only the listed scripts.

Cookie
a small file that a site saves in the browser.

a random identifier of a DeiX OS installation; not related to hardware serial numbers.

22. Questions and answers

Can you see my password?

No. The password is sent over a protected connection at sign-in or registration and is then stored only as a hash. It cannot be read or recovered; a forgotten password can only be replaced with a new one.

Can you read the names of my devices and my key history?

No. They live in your browser, encrypted with a key derived from your password, and are not sent to the server. The exception: a device name that you yourself sent in a dev-mode key request is visible to the operator.

What does the Owner see?

In the request list: the username, the device identifier and name, the note, the source, the status and the times. The list does not show your email, your password or the contents of your vault.

Why is there a captcha and what does Cloudflare receive?

So that bots cannot register en masse and guess passwords. Cloudflare receives the IP address and browser information, as described in section 10.

Why is there no “Accept cookies” banner?

We use only the cookies necessary for signing in. The account cannot work without them, and we have no advertising or analytics records.

I forgot my password. Will the vault data be lost?

The vault is encrypted with the password and cannot be recovered. Reset it on the Account page: you will lose the device names and key history, but the keys already in the OS will keep working.

Does the OS send anything without my command?

No. The only background network activity relates to your key request: the OS polls the status until you get a decision. See section 15.

How do I delete my account?

Write to the operator (section 20). The profile, device links, code hashes and the record with the password hash are deleted together with the account. See section 14.

Do you pass data to third parties?

Only to the infrastructure providers in section 10 and only to the extent needed to run the Service. We sell nothing.
